Our wines

OOST is named for the south-easter. Every summer afternoon it drags a cloud over Table Mountain and pours it down onto our vines, and the rows stay seven degrees cooler than the city on the other side.

Our story

A family farm on the cold side of the mountain since 1891.

Capetonians call the cloud the tablecloth. From town it looks like a postcard. From our rows it's a cold front that arrives at about two o'clock and stays until dark.

It's the reason we exist. The grapes ripen slowly and keep their acidity in a place that should be too hot for them. We grow 34 hectares, pick by hand at dawn, and make about 60,000 bottles a year in the cellar at the bottom of the slope.

Anna Visser, winemaker
