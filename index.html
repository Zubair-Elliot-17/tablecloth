The farm on the cold side of the mountain

Tablecloth is a family wine farm in Constantia, on the far side of Table Mountain from the city. Vines have grown on this slope since 1891. Most summer afternoons the south-easter drags a cloud over the top of the mountain, and Capetonians call it the tablecloth. From town it looks like a postcard. From our rows it is a cold front that arrives at about two o'clock and stays until dark.

That cloud is the reason we exist. It keeps the vineyard around seven degrees cooler than the city, so the grapes ripen slowly and keep their acidity in a place that should be too hot for them. We grow 34 hectares, pick by hand at dawn, make about 60,000 bottles a year in the cellar at the bottom of the slope, and pour them at the cellar door six days a week.

I.The cloud

Why our side of the mountain stays cool

The south-easter blows across False Bay in summer and picks up moisture on the way. When it hits Table Mountain it is pushed up the slopes, cools, and condenses into a flat white cloud that sits on the summit. Then it spills over the edge and pours down the other side, evaporating as it sinks into warmer air.

Constantia lies in the lee of that pour. While the City Bowl bakes at thirty degrees, our rows sit under cloud and wind. The grapes keep their freshness, and the wines taste of a cooler place than a map of Africa would suggest.

II.The slope

Five blocks, one cellar

We don't buy in grapes. Every bottle comes from 34 hectares on the slope below the mountain, split into five blocks that each make their own wine. The highest rows sit under the cloud most summer afternoons. The lowest catch more sun and give us riper fruit to blend with.

Each block is picked on the day it tastes ready, by hand and at dawn, then driven a few hundred metres down the farm tracks to the cellar. Scroll the map to watch a harvest come in, or pick a block to read about it.
